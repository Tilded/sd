# How to Play Shadowdark Across the Western Reaches

**Hierarchy.** 01 is the core engine. 02 adds player-facing Western Reaches options and alternate setting procedures. 03 is the GM-facing companion and selects when those alternatives apply. Unless otherwise noted, use the core rules in 01. (03, p. 29)

**Citation convention.** Page numbers use the explicit page anchors in the supplied Markdown files. All rules claims cite their source book and page. Adaptation proposals are labelled as such.

## 1. Core engine: inputs → resolution → state change → feedback

### Inputs: intent, situation, and character

A player states what their character attempts. The GM frames what the character can perceive, determines what is at stake, adjudicates the world, and describes the result. The game does not require a check for every action. Routine tasks that fall within a character's training normally succeed when there is no meaningful uncertainty. A check is appropriate when failure has a negative consequence, the action requires skill, or time pressure matters. (01, pp. 11, 84)

The six stats are Strength, Dexterity, Constitution, Intelligence, Wisdom, and Charisma. Stats normally range from 3–18 and each has a modifier from −4 to +4. The GM chooses the relevant stat based on the task. Wisdom covers perception and instinct; Intelligence covers knowledge and logic. (01, pp. 12, 18, 83)

### Resolution: checks, attacks, and spells

**Stat check.** Roll 1d20, add the relevant stat modifier, and compare the total with the GM's difficulty class (DC). The action succeeds if the total equals or exceeds the DC. Standard DCs are Easy 9, Normal 12, Hard 15, and Extreme 18. (01, pp. 12, 84, 110)

**Contested check.** Each opposing creature rolls the relevant check simultaneously, even if it is not that creature's turn. The highest result wins; ties are rerolled. (01, p. 84)

**Advantage/disadvantage.** Roll twice and use the better result for advantage or the worse result for disadvantage. Apply these where a rule or sound ruling calls for them, rather than creating a new modifier for every circumstance. (01, p. 12)

**Attack.** A melee attack is 1d20 + Strength modifier + applicable talent bonuses against the target's AC. A ranged attack uses Dexterity instead. A result equal to or greater than AC hits. On a hit, roll weapon or spell damage dice plus applicable bonuses and subtract the result from HP. (01, pp. 91–92)

**Critical hit.** A natural 20 on an attack roll or spellcasting check is a critical hit. Double a weapon's damage dice, or double one numerical effect of a spell. (01, p. 92)

**Spellcasting.** Casting a spell takes the caster's action. Wizards roll 1d20 + Intelligence modifier; priests roll 1d20 + Wisdom modifier. The DC is 10 + the spell's tier. Success produces the spell's effect. A failed spell does not take effect and cannot be cast again until the caster completes a rest. A natural 1 is a critical failure: wizard spells also trigger the appropriate wizard mishap; priest spells require penance and a successful rest before being cast again. (01, pp. 47–50)

A focus spell requires a spellcasting check at the start of the caster's turn to maintain it. Failure ends the spell. A caster cannot cast another focus spell while already focusing. (01, p. 53)

### State change: turns, rounds, movement, and time

**Time.** A round is complete once every participant has taken a turn. The basic procedure has the GM begin and turns proceeding clockwise; the advanced procedure sets initiative. If the table cannot track real time, one in-game hour may be treated as ten rounds. (01, pp. 11–12, 85–86)

**Initiative.** At the start of play, each participant rolls d20 + Dexterity modifier. The GM uses the highest relevant monster Dexterity modifier. The highest result starts, and order proceeds clockwise. A freeform round-robin is also permitted. (01, p. 86)

**Player turn.** Count down personal timers, then take one action and move up to near, splitting movement around the action as desired. A character who does not take an action may move near again. The GM describes the result. (01, p. 86)

**GM turn.** Count down timers not tracked by players, check for a random encounter when required, move and act for relevant creatures or environmental effects, and describe what happens. (01, p. 86)

**Distances.** Close is 5 feet; near is up to 30 feet; far is within sight during an encounter or scene. These are intentionally loose categories. (01, p. 88)

**Crawling and combat.** Exploration outside combat uses crawling rounds. When a fight begins, combat rounds start. Surprise is resolved first, then combat initiative. (01, pp. 87, 91)

**Combat action economy.** A character normally takes one action and moves near. Actions include melee attack, ranged attack, casting a spell, and improvising. Small parallel tasks such as speaking, standing, activating an item, or quaffing a potion usually do not consume the action. A character may move near again instead of taking an action. (01, pp. 91–92)

**Surprise and stealth.** A creature that begins its turn undetected can take a surprise turn before initiative is rerolled. Hiding and sneaking ordinarily use Dexterity checks. A searcher actively looking or listening in the right place reveals a hidden creature automatically; otherwise a Wisdom check may be required. (01, p. 90)

### Combat consequences and survival

Attacking or casting at a creature with at least half its body behind interposing terrain imposes disadvantage; a creature completely obscured cannot be targeted. Movement through terrain that hampers free movement is halved. Enemies reduced to half their number, or a solo enemy reduced to half HP, flee if they fail a DC 15 Wisdom check. (01, p. 92)

At 0 HP a character falls unconscious and is dying. On that turn, roll 1d4 + Constitution modifier, minimum 1, to set the number of rounds before death. On later turns, a natural 20 on a d20 returns the character to 1 HP. An intelligent being at close range can attempt first aid with a DC 15 Intelligence check; success stabilizes the target but leaves them unconscious. A character who dies is retired from play. (01, p. 92)

### Resource flows: light, gear, rest, magic, and XP

**Light is a clock.** Most light sources last about one hour of real time and illuminate a limited area. Outside that area is total darkness. Non-dark-adapted creatures in total darkness have disadvantage on tasks requiring sight, and the environment is deadly: the GM checks for random encounters every crawling round. (01, pp. 87–88)

**Gear capacity.** A character carries a number of items equal to their Strength score or 10, whichever is higher. Most gear takes one gear slot; typical clothing does not. Gear slots make light, rations, tools, rope, and treasure compete for capacity. (01, pp. 16, 38–39)

**Rest.** A successful rest requires a ration and eight hours of sleep. Each stressful interruption requires a DC 12 Constitution check. On failure the ration is consumed but no rest benefit is gained. On success, regain all lost HP, recover stat damage, and restore abilities that specify a successful rest. In dangerous areas, check for encounters every three hours (Unsafe), two hours (Risky), or one hour (Deadly). (01, p. 89)

**Spell resources.** Known spells are determined by class and level. A failed spell is unavailable until rest; a wizard critical failure also creates a mishap, while a priest critical failure invokes penance. Focus spells need continued checks. Scrolls and wands use the tier-based spellcasting procedure with their own item rules. (01, pp. 47–53)

**Luck tokens.** The GM may award a luck token for exceptional roleplaying, heroism, or a daring act. A player may hold only one at a time. Spending it rerolls a roll just made, and the new result must be used; it can instead be given to a companion. (01, p. 82)

**XP and advancement.** The default reward loop is treasure and boons, not monster kills. Treasure quality awards Poor 0 XP, Normal 1 XP, Fabulous 3 XP, or Legendary 10 XP; every PC receives the full award. A new level requires current level × 10 XP; XP resets to zero after leveling. The advancement table grants talent rolls at levels 1, 3, 5, 7, and 9, and maximum HP increases by one class HP die at each level. (01, pp. 42, 120)

### Character creation

A character record includes name, ancestry, class, level, XP, alignment and deity, title, background, stats, HP, AC, attacks, talents, spells, and gear. (01, pp. 15–16)

Roll 3d6 in order for each of the six stats. If none is 14 or higher, the player may optionally reroll the entire set. (01, p. 18)

A 0-level start uses stats, ancestry, Constitution-modifier HP (minimum 1), background, alignment, and starting gear. A 0-level character can use all gear through beginner's luck and reaches 1st level after surviving the first adventure. A 1st-level start adds a class, a class talent roll, one class HP die plus Constitution modifier (minimum 1), and a title. (01, p. 17)

Core ancestries are dwarf, elf, goblin, half-orc, halfling, and human. Core classes are fighter, priest, thief, and wizard. Class entries define weapon and armor permissions, HP die, class features, and talents; priests and wizards also have known-spell progressions. (01, pp. 19–28)

Backgrounds represent past experience and may grant advantage when relevant. Alignment is Lawful, Chaotic, or Neutral. Priests choose a deity compatible with alignment; a deity may revoke spellcasting after blasphemy. (01, pp. 29–31)

## 2. Player-facing expansion: 02

### Character options

02 adds Western Reaches variants for dwarf, elf, goblin, half-elf, half-orc, halfling, human, and kobold ancestries, plus new backgrounds, secrets, and class titles. These additions use the core character sheet, stats, HP, AC, gear slots, and check engine. (02, pp. 17–32, 73–82)

The added classes are bard, basilisk warrior, delver, desert rider, duelist, green knight, knight of St. Ydris, Kyzian archer, monk of Yag-Kesh, necromancer, paladin, pit fighter, ranger, Ras-Godai, roustabout, sea wolf, seer, warlock, witch, and wyrdling. Each class defines its own weapon/armor permissions, HP die, talents, and any spellcasting or limited-use features. (02, pp. 33–71)

02 adds necromancer, expanded priest, seer, witch, and additional wizard spell material, plus Diabolical and Necromancer mishap tables. These still use the tier/range/duration framework and the spellcasting-check interface unless a spell says otherwise. (02, pp. 121–185; 01, pp. 47–53)

### Factions, renown, downtime, bases

A character joins one faction through an initiation ceremony and remains in good standing by following its bylaws. A faction leader has final authority or may submit disputes to majority vote. A peaceful leadership transfer requires a DC 15 Charisma check; failure splits the faction into two organizations. A departing PC loses benefits. Benefits depend on the combined levels of party members in the faction, and only members receive them. (02, pp. 89–90)

The six factions are The Bards, The Jeweled Eye, The Scarlet Bones, The Society, The Torchbearers, and The Wolves of Lydonia. Each has missions, bylaws, leadership, rumor access, local presence, and benefits at aggregate membership thresholds. The GM guide supplies setting counterparts and local actors; see the source bible. (02, pp. 91–103; 03, pp. 17–26)

Renown begins equal to a PC's Charisma modifier. It rises through level gains, public honors, extravagant spending, and major triumphs; it falls through public humiliation, trouble with the law, fashion mistakes, cultural faux pas, or offending higher-status people. Renown modifies carousing and some reaction rolls. (02, p. 232)

02 adds downtime activities including spiritualism, skulduggery, martial training, and magical research. A PC gets one activity between adventures. A failed check lowers the next attempt's DC one step, resetting after success. Luck tokens cannot be used on these checks. (02, pp. 233–235)

Expanded carousing uses an event cost and bonus, then a 1d8 result to assign XP and rolls on benefit/mishap tables. Renown, holidays, bard talents, clothing, and site can modify results. (02, pp. 235–244) 03 caps event size by settlement: village 100 gp, town 600 gp, city 1,200 gp, city-state no cap; the GM may instead use core carousing. (03, p. 29)

Bastions add bases with AC, HP, construction, repairs, upgrades, safety, and monthly disaster checks. Warbands add a mass-combat unit layer: groups of up to 20 similar combatants treated as one creature, with recruiting, maintenance, upgrades, command limits, initiative, and morale. (02, pp. 245–250)

### Reaches travel and climate

The Reaches hex crawl uses six-mile hexes and group movement. Check encounters twice by day and twice overnight at 1:6 each; a d12 determines time. Daily weather uses a d6: stormy weather worsens terrain; excellent weather grants advantage on the next weather roll. Base movement is four hexes walking, six mounted, and eight sailing per day, adjusted by terrain cost. Each PC and mount consumes a ration per day. Pushing multiplies movement by 1.5, adds an additional 1:6 encounter chance, and prevents foraging that day. (02, pp. 225–226)

Visibility depends on adjacent hexes and conditions. Climate varies by region and season. Harsh climate requires double rations or counts as no food; foraging/hunting is DC 18 in normal weather and impossible in stormy weather. Seasonal underground exposure can cause Charisma damage. (02, pp. 226–228)

Camping is an optional task layer during rest: each PC can perform one task, typically at DC 12, while still receiving a full rest. Resolve tasks first, then normal rest and encounter procedures. Fast travel is limited to previously explored routes; the GM estimates time and required rations. A group without the required rations may arrive Drained. (02, pp. 229–231)

### Gear and transport

02 adds equipment while retaining the gear-slot economy unless an entry says otherwise. Mount rules cover rarity, gear capacity, movement, personality, combat, leveling, and feeding. A rider and mount act on the rider's initiative; either may use the action. (02, pp. 105–116)

Boats define passenger capacity, repairs, speed, sinking, slots, and properties. Siege weapons define ammunition, assembly, slots, operation, range, damage, and properties. (02, pp. 117–119)

## 3. GM-facing expansion: 03

### Rule merge and explicit overrides

03 directs the GM to use 01 unless otherwise noted. It selects 02's alternate Reaches hex crawl, climate, and downtime; adds Reaches terrain encounter tables; adopts modified boats and mounts; and applies Hard Luck Mode. Under Hard Luck Mode, luck tokens cannot be spent on critical failures or effects that generate luck, such as bless. (03, p. 29)

**Override:** In a Western Reaches campaign using both guides, use the 02 Reaches travel procedure instead of 01's core overland cadence. This replaces the travel movement and encounter schedule only; it does not replace the core check, turn, attack, or rest engine. Outside the Reaches, 01 remains the baseline. (01, pp. 93–94; 02, pp. 225–231; 03, p. 29)

**Override:** Use the Reaches climate, camping, and fast-travel procedures when those situations arise. These add setting-specific costs and tasks to the core rest/check structure. (01, p. 89; 02, pp. 227–231; 03, p. 29)

**Override:** Hard Luck Mode restricts the ordinary luck-token spend. It does not say that luck tokens are removed from play entirely. (01, p. 82; 03, p. 29)

### Campaign procedures and setting material

03 describes sandbox play, rumor seeding, multiple PCs, and a West Marches option with open-table scheduling, safe havens, return-to-safety procedures, and campaign time tracking. These are campaign-management options, not replacements for the character turn or check procedure. (03, pp. 30–34)

03 also covers starting areas and home bases, mission-based sessions, adventure hooks, improvisation, and note-keeping. Suggested notes include open threads, current PC locations/actions, rumors, important NPCs, and major world developments. (03, pp. 35–36)

The GM guide defines the five city-states and their overlords, resources, laws, factions, and notable sites; the sixteen regions; Morzomotha; the Master Hex Key; regional encounters and sites; City of Masks districts; and setting-specific creatures/NPCs. Use the page-anchored rows in 03_source_bible.md as the reference index. (03, pp. 15–26, 67–83, 248–282)

The setting deliberately leaves small geographical features unnamed and allows the GM to add new material. An unnamed pond, ruin, or local feature is design space, not a missing canon fact. (03, pp. 13–14, 35–36)

## 4. Minimum viable play loop

1. **Frame the situation.** State what the character perceives, what is at stake, and visible danger. Distances use close/near/far. (01, pp. 87–88, 108)
2. **Declare an action.** The player says what the character attempts. Do not roll for routine trained actions without consequence. (01, pp. 11, 84)
3. **Resolve if needed.** Choose a stat and DC; roll d20 + modifier. In combat, attack against AC; for spells, use the caster's stat and DC 10 + tier. (01, pp. 47, 84, 91)
4. **Apply state change.** Update position, HP, conditions, item use, timers, spell availability, or NPC attitude as appropriate. (01, pp. 47–53, 86, 91–92, 115–118)
5. **Advance the world.** Resolve the GM turn, count timers, make required encounter checks, and describe the new situation. (01, pp. 85–87, 115–116)
6. **Preserve resource pressure.** Track light, gear slots, and rations. After danger, use the eight-hour ration-backed rest. Award XP for treasure/boons and level up at current level × 10 XP. (01, pp. 38–39, 42, 82, 89, 120)

For a Western Reaches prototype, add the 02 hex-travel, weather, climate, camping, and fast-travel procedures plus 03's setting encounter tables; retain the core action/check/combat loop. (02, pp. 225–231; 03, pp. 29, 39–64)
