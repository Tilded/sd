# Adapting Other Systems to Shadowdark

**Direction:** Convert external material *into* Shadowdark. Preserve the destination engine's action economy, stats, DC/AC comparisons, resource clocks, consequences, and reward loop. Do not keep foreign arithmetic merely because it is already printed in a stat block.

**Evidence boundary:** Statements about Shadowdark below are source facts with page citations. Conversion choices are labelled as *adaptation proposals* and are not new rules in the books.

## 1. Invariants of this system

1. **Intent precedes mechanics.** Players describe actions. The GM calls for a check when failure has a negative consequence, the action requires skill, or time pressure applies. Routine trained tasks normally succeed without a check. (01, pp. 11, 84)
2. **One common check language.** A stat check is 1d20 + the linked stat modifier against a DC; equality succeeds. Standard DCs are 9, 12, 15, and 18. Contested checks are simultaneous; highest wins; ties reroll. (01, p. 84)
3. **One action is the PC combat budget.** A PC generally takes one action and moves near on their turn; they may move near again instead of taking an action. Small parallel tasks are normally multitasks. (01, pp. 86, 91)
4. **Shadowdark distances are coarse.** Close, near, and far replace most foot-by-foot tactical measurement. (01, p. 88)
5. **AC and HP are shared interfaces.** Attacks roll against AC; damage dice plus bonuses reduce HP. (01, pp. 91–92)
6. **Magic uses tiers and checks.** Spells have tier, range, duration and sometimes focus; casting DC is 10 + tier. Failure and natural 1 have explicit recovery/mishap consequences. (01, pp. 47–53)
7. **Time and light create pressure.** Light usually runs on real time; crawling rounds, timers, and random encounters make delay costly. (01, pp. 85–89, 115)
8. **Inventory choices matter.** Gear slots are limited by Strength or 10, whichever is higher; most gear consumes a slot. (01, pp. 16, 38–39)
9. **Recovery is ration-backed and time-consuming.** Standard rest needs a ration and eight hours of sleep; interruption may consume the ration without benefit. (01, p. 89)
10. **Treasure and boons drive default XP.** Poor/Normal/Fabulous/Legendary treasure awards 0/1/3/10 XP; all PCs receive the full amount. Monster defeat is not the default XP source; Hunter Mode is an optional variation. (01, pp. 114, 120)

These are the destination system's load-bearing commitments. An import that retains foreign bonus-action chains, spell slots, encounter-day pacing, proficiency arithmetic, or monster-CR math without reconciliation is still running two systems.

## 3. Stepwise procedure

### Step 1 — Identify the content's job

Classify the external material as a creature/stat block, player option, spell, item, trap/hazard, NPC/faction, location, encounter, or campaign procedure. Identify what decision, risk, resource cost, or discovery it creates. Do not turn descriptive lore into a mechanic merely because it has a paragraph.

### Step 2 — Strip away foreign action labels

Write down what happens on the creature's or character's turn. Reassign the primary effect to one Shadowdark action. A foreign bonus action, reaction, legendary action, or multi-part action can become:
- the creature's one action;
- a small multitask that normally does not consume an action;
- a response resolved on the GM's turn;
- or an explicitly labelled adaptation proposal.

Do not silently grant extra actions. PCs default to one action plus near movement. Monsters can have one or two attacks per turn, and some can have more; this is a stat-block choice within the system, not permission to import a second action economy. (01, pp. 86, 91, 192)

### Step 3 — Convert stats into modifiers

Map foreign ability scores to STR/DEX/CON/INT/WIS/CHA and use the Shadowdark modifier table. Convert proficiency or expertise into an existing talent bonus, advantage on the relevant check, or the rule that routine trained activity succeeds without a check. Mark any new numerical bonus as a proposal. (01, pp. 18, 83–84)

### Step 4 — Rebuild the creature chassis

For a monster, decide its LV relative to the party and role; begin AC at party level + 10 as suggested by the monster generator; calculate HP using d8s per LV plus CON modifier, minimum 1, or use the listed average; set stat modifiers, attack bonus, attacks, damage, movement, alignment, and one to three talents. There is no source-defined universal 5e CR-to-LV equation. (01, pp. 193–196)

The monster design roles are Mook, Soldier, Striker, Tank, Controller, and Legendary. The level bands inform expected HP, damage dice, number of attacks, and treasure. The attack bonus should rarely exceed the monster's level. (01, pp. 195–196)

### Step 5 — Convert special abilities by function

For each trait, ask what decision it creates. Position effects become movement and distance rules. Task bonuses become advantage/disadvantage or a check. Damage features become dice and triggers. Behavior becomes reaction or morale. Resource effects must state their trigger, duration, cost, and recovery. Effects without a Shadowdark interface remain open design work; label them proposals.

### Step 6 — Convert spells and magical effects

Assign a tier, caster stat, range, duration, and whether focus is required. Set the casting DC to 10 + tier. Define failure behavior using the relevant class's own rules. Do not automatically preserve foreign spell slots, concentration, or restoration. (01, pp. 47–53)

### Step 7 — Convert adventure pressure

For each scene, identify the real pressure: light duration, time, random encounters, noise, rations, gear capacity, hazard, reaction, or consequence. Give dangers tells where appropriate. A trap should have a discoverable tell; searching the correct area reveals it, and disabling succeeds when there is enough time and a reasonable method. Reserve checks for skill/time pressure. (01, pp. 87–90, 108, 115–118)

For a Reaches journey, use the 02 hex, weather, climate, camping, rations, and fast-travel procedures because 03 selects them for the setting. Do not run the core overland schedule and Reaches schedule simultaneously. (02, pp. 225–231; 03, p. 29)

### Step 8 — Convert the reward loop

Translate gold, treasure, magic items, secrets, vows, and boons into the Poor/Normal/Fabulous/Legendary XP-quality categories. Award each PC the full value. Convert milestone or monster XP only by a clear campaign decision or an explicit option such as Hunter Mode. (01, pp. 114, 120)

### Step 9 — Stress test the conversion

Verify that the content fits the intended action economy; attacks target plausible AC; HP and damage fit LV and role; abilities have triggers, targets, range, duration, and recovery; spells have tier, range, duration, and failure behavior; encounters offer information and meaningful choices; and no borrowed subsystem bypasses light, gear slots, rations, rest, morale, or treasure XP.

Record unresolved values as open, actual source contradictions as conflicted, and deliberate replacements as override.

## 4. Worked example — 5e-style goblin

This is an adaptation proposal. The input is a representative 5e-style goblin profile, not a quotation from either Shadowdark guide.

### Illustrative external input

| Field | Input |
|---|---|
| AC / HP | AC 15; 7 HP |
| Movement | 30 feet |
| Ability scores | STR 8, DEX 14, CON 10, INT 10, WIS 8, CHA 8 |
| Attacks | Scimitar +4, 1d6+2; shortbow +4, 1d6+2 |
| Special trait | Nimble Escape: hide or disengage as a bonus action |
| Role | Small, evasive skirmisher |

### Shadowdark destination profile

The core book already supplies a Shadowdark goblin stat block, which is the destination baseline for this archetype:

> **Goblin (source profile, not a conversion formula):** AC 11, HP 5, ATK 1 club +0 (1d4) or 1 shortbow (far) +1 (1d4), MV near, S +0, D +1, C +1, I −1, W −1, Ch −2, AL C, LV 1. **Keen Senses:** can't be surprised. (01, p. 224)

### Mapping notes

- **Ability scale:** the six input ability concepts map to the six Shadowdark stats. A 5e DEX 14 maps to DEX +2 on the modifier table, but the published Shadowdark goblin uses DEX +1. This is target-system calibration, not a universal conversion formula. (01, pp. 18, 224)
- **AC and HP:** do not retain AC 15 / HP 7 automatically. Use the target's level and role model; the published goblin is LV 1, AC 11, HP 5. The monster generator's baseline AC is party level + 10. (01, pp. 193, 195–196, 224)
- **Attacks:** shortbow remains a far-ranged attack, but attack bonus and damage dice are recalibrated. Retain the scimitar only if the adventure requires that weapon identity; the core goblin profile uses a club and shortbow. (01, pp. 40, 224)
- **Nimble Escape:** a 5e bonus action cannot be imported as a free second action. **Proposal:** express evasiveness as advantage on a Dexterity check to hide when a plausible hiding place exists, or require the goblin to spend its action to hide/disengage. This is a conversion choice, not a feature printed in the core goblin block. (01, pp. 84, 86, 90–91)

The result uses one Shadowdark stat block, one action economy, near movement, and the system's reaction/morale procedures. It does not retain foreign bonus-action timing or numeric bonuses.

## 5. Adaptation boundaries

**Immutable:** core check logic, PC action budget, distance language, light/time pressure, gear slots, spellcasting-check interface, rest requirements, and default treasure/boon XP loop. (01, pp. 38–39, 42, 47–53, 82, 84–89, 91)

**Adaptable:** names, appearance, culture, factions, regions, creature identity, weapon choice, spell fiction, and the tuning of a new stat block when calibrated to level and role.

**Open:** exact conversion ratios the books do not give, especially foreign CR to Shadowdark LV, proficiency bonus to talent bonus, short-rest resources, and conditions with no Shadowdark equivalent.

**Conflicted:** use this label only when source statements actually disagree. The Reaches travel procedure is an override, not a contradiction: 03 explicitly selects the 02 alternative for that setting. (01, pp. 93–94; 02, pp. 225–231; 03, p. 29)
